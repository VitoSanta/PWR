# Redesign — evaluation by harness area, models without a probe, a local engine

**Status: PROPOSED, 2026-09-17, with its first decisions taken the same day
(see Decisions). Nothing here is implemented; the open decisions at the end are
still to be settled.**

## Why

On 2026-09-17 R3's H2 development run was stopped after 35 of 79 trials. The
run did what a development run is for -- every task forced at least two
compactions, all 17 injections landed where intended, and two harness defects
were found and fixed -- but it made visible a mistake in how every campaign so
far has been set up:

- **One operating point answered every question.** Every calibrated campaign
  since R2 ran at a 16,384-token window, the highest tier calibration admitted
  for both Qwen 3.6 35B and GLM 4.7 Flash on this Mac (Qwen's first token at
  24 s at 16,384). That number came from calibration, not from
  any question being asked, and it then became the window for measuring tool
  calls, navigation, edits, verification, compaction and task completion at
  once.
- **At that window, completion measures starvation.** The history budget is
  half the window less the learned prompt overhead (about 3,000 tokens of tool
  schemas and chat template), so roughly 5,000 tokens. 2 of 35 trials resolved;
  33 stopped on the action budget or the stall guard; one task compacted 29
  times in 40 actions. A failure there mixes context starvation with model and
  harness defects, and no completion rate from it says what PWR does for a
  user with a normal context.
- **An external reference disagrees.** The user reports LM Studio's own harness
  (Bionic) working well on coding tasks with qwen3-coder-next at 3-bit and a
  moderate context. Not measured here; it is the reason to test at a realistic
  window rather than assume the local model is the limit.

The fix is not one larger-window run. It is to section evaluation so that each
campaign tests one area of the harness, at the window and with the metrics that
area needs.

## Results so far are "under the old regime"

Every campaign up to and including `experiments/r3-h2-dev-20260917` is labelled
**old regime** (*sotto vecchia gestione*): one fixed calibrated window, one
blended completion rate, a calibration probe before any run, and models served
through Ollama or LM Studio.

What stays valid from them:

- **Defects found and fixed** (D1–D8, the verifier allowlist and evidence-window
  fixes of 2026-09-17), each with its fixture. They were harness bugs whatever
  the window.
- **Mechanism observations**: re-reads after compaction (208 of 226 re-reads of
  files a compaction folded away, 2026-09-16), injection timing, seed variance
  (3 of 12 task-deployment pairs flip on the seed). These are properties of the
  loop under pressure, which is what a small window legitimately creates.
- **Infrastructure**: the corpora and their gates (`check-corpus`, wrong
  implementations, injections), the evaluator, pairing, traces, the analysis
  scripts.

What is not to be read from them:

- **Any completion rate as a statement of capability.** R2's "no uplift" (B1 18
  vs B0 13 on Qwen, p = 0.23) and the harness-revision result (14 vs 17) were
  measured at 16,384 tokens and describe that regime only.
- **Sample sizes derived from them.** The ~97 paired tasks and the 29–50 host
  hours for R3 come from old-regime discordance and completion rates.

`docs/roadmap.md` and `docs/experiment-log.md` carry the label from 2026-09-17.

## Part A — evaluation sectioned by harness area

Every campaign names its area. Each area has its own tasks, window, metrics and
gate; a completion rate is a metric only in the end-to-end area.

| Area | Question | Tasks | Window | Primary metrics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 Tool calls** | Does the deployment produce valid calls, and does the harness recover when it does not? | Short scripted and single-file tasks; recorded malformed outputs replayed | Small (8–16k) | Valid-call rate, malformed calls by kind, turns lost to a malformed call, recovery rate |
| **A2 Navigation** | Does the run find the code the change belongs in without wasted reads? | Diagnosis and "where is X" tasks on large repositories, known answer | Medium (32k) | Correct location found, actions and tokens to find it, re-reads of unchanged files |
| **A3 Editing** | Are edits applied as intended and kept in scope? | Single- and multi-file changes with given locations | Medium | Edits applied first time, escape and whitespace failures, out-of-scope changes |
| **A4 Verification** | Does the run run the right checks, read them, and refuse to claim a false completion? | Tasks with failing, flaky, slow and misleading checks | Medium | Checks run, false completions, verifier adoption, completions declared against red checks |
| **A5 Context and compaction** | Does compaction keep what the run needs? (H2 lives here) | `longhorizon-v2` and its injected variants | **Small on purpose**, to force compactions | Re-reads after compaction, revisions lost, stale evidence delivered, evidence windows; **not** completion |
| **A6 End to end** | Does PWR finish real tasks a user would give it? | Realistic tasks from real repositories, mixed kinds | **Realistic** (64k or the deployment's comfortable maximum) | Resolved rate, cost, wall time, human-rated legibility of the run |

Rules:

1. **A campaign's claim is scoped to its area.** A5 can show that a policy keeps
   evidence; only A6 can show that it helps users finish tasks.
2. **A task belongs to one area.** A task used in A5 at a small window is not
   reused as an A6 completion sample.
3. **Window is a condition, recorded, never inherited.** Each area declares it;
   A6 may sweep it.
4. **Areas A1–A4 are fast by design** (minutes per trial), so a harness change
   can be checked against them the same day.
5. **An external baseline enters A6.** The same A6 tasks run by hand in another
   harness (Bionic with the same model, first) give a reference that says
   whether a gap is PWR's.

Existing corpora map as follows, to be re-audited per area rather than moved
wholesale: `external-v1/v2` mostly A3/A6, `m6-hard-v1` A2/A4, `longhorizon-v1/v2`
A5, `realistic-v1` and `longhaul-v1` A6.

## Part B — loading a model without a probe

Today `pwr eval` and the chat console require a calibration profile, produced
by `pwr calibrate`, which fills the context at a ladder of sizes, measures
first-token latency, generation rate and needle recall, and admits the tiers
that pass. It exists for two historical reasons: LM Studio ignored the
requested window for Qwen 3.5/3.6 on MLX, and the window was chosen by latency.

Proposed, in the manner of LM Studio:

- **The model's maximum** comes from its metadata (GGUF/MLX config, or the
  backend's model listing), not from filling it.
- **The working window** is a setting with a default per model size and memory,
  which the user can change; the app shows its cost in memory.
- **Speed is observed, not probed**: first-token latency and generation rate are
  recorded from real turns and shown; a window that proves unusable is reported
  from those measurements.
- **Campaigns record** model digest, quantisation, engine and version, window
  and sampling -- the reproducibility the profile gave, without the probe.
- **Calibration stays as an optional diagnostic** (`pwr calibrate`), not a
  gate on loading a model.

**Needle recall -- keep or drop.** Calibration also checked that a needle placed
in a full window came back exactly.

- *For keeping it*: it catches a window that is silently not honoured or
  truncated (it did, with LM Studio and Qwen on MLX); it catches models whose
  recall collapses at long context -- RoPE scaling past the trained length,
  sliding-window layers, aggressive quantisation -- which is exactly what a
  computed "largest window" risks; and it is cheap evidence for the catalogue.
- *For dropping it*: filling a large window is slow on this hardware (minutes
  at 64k and beyond) and a bad experience at load time; a needle is a weak proxy
  for using long context in reasoning, so passing it proves little; and with an
  embedded engine that sets the window and counts tokens itself, the truncation
  failure it caught cannot happen silently.
- *Proposed middle*: never at load time; run once per (model, quantisation,
  engine, window) when an entry enters the catalogue, and available as a
  diagnostic. A failure lowers the catalogued window for that entry.

## Part C — a local engine instead of a provider

### What the data say about providers

In the 35 trials of 2026-09-17 there was **one malformed call in 1,027 turns**
(`multiple_calls`: the model emitted two calls in one turn) and it was the
model's, not the provider's. Malformed calls fell from 15 to 4 across the
harness repairs of 2026-09-16. Malformed calls are therefore not, today, a
reason to leave Ollama and LM Studio. The reasons are control, speed and
self-containment.

### What an embedded engine gives

- **Calls that cannot be malformed**: grammar- or schema-constrained decoding of
  tool calls, and our own chat template and parser per model family, instead of
  whatever the provider's server applies.
- **Prompt cache under our control**: KV-cache reuse across turns, so a turn
  prefills only what changed. Prefill is today's bottleneck (24 s first token
  at 16,384) and is what makes realistic windows slow.
- **Exact window and sampling**, set by us and read back, with nothing to
  calibrate around.
- **A self-contained app**: no second application to install and keep running.

What it costs: templates, parsers and stop handling maintained per model family;
model download, storage and memory management; following upstream engine
releases; a second place where a local-model bug can live.

### Platforms

MLX runs only on Apple Silicon; the app is for macOS and Windows. Decided
2026-09-17: **MLX first**, because development happens on macOS, with the
boundary built so Windows is not designed out.

- **An `Engine` boundary** in `pwr-provider`, beside today's HTTP backends:
  load a model at a window, tokenise, apply the chat template, generate with a
  prompt cache and optional constrained decoding, report memory and speed. Its
  tests run against a fake engine, so nothing above it knows which engine ran.
- **MLX as the first embedded engine**, on macOS.
- **llama.cpp (GGUF) as the Windows engine**, built behind the same boundary
  before the Windows build (S3); it also runs on macOS, which lets the two be
  compared on one machine.
- **Ollama and LM Studio stay as optional backends**, not the default.

A spike comes first: one model through MLX in-process and through today's LM
Studio path, on this Mac, measured for prefill and generation at 16k, 32k, 64k
and beyond, prompt-cache reuse across turns, and constrained tool-call decoding.
How PWR's Rust core reaches MLX (a Python sidecar over `mlx-lm`, or the MLX C
API through bindings) is the spike's first question.

### Spike result, 2026-09-17

[`experiments/engine-spike-mlx-20260917`](../experiments/engine-spike-mlx-20260917/analysis.md)
measured Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 4-bit from 4k to 256k tokens: MLX in-process, the
same MLX build through LM Studio, and GGUF through LM Studio's llama.cpp.

- **Speed does not argue for an embedded engine.** LM Studio's MLX path
  prefilled faster than the spike's in-process code and generated within about a
  quarter of it; cache reuse across turns already works through LM Studio.
- MLX generates faster than llama.cpp on this machine (56 against 29 tokens/s at
  64k); llama.cpp prefills slightly faster at small windows.
- The whole 262k window fits in 64 GB (49 GB peak). Cache cost matches the
  config formula (20.5 KB per token), but the peak is dominated by a prefill
  transient about four times the cache, which Part D's computation must include.
- The old regime's calibration understated LM Studio badly (7 tokens/s recorded
  at 16k; 44 GGUF and 65 MLX measured today).

So the case for an embedded engine is control and self-containment, not speed,
and LM Studio's MLX path is a sound default until that control is needed.
Whether and when to build the engine is reopened as a decision.

### Weights from HuggingFace, and why it is the same decision

Raised 2026-09-17: dropping the requirement to have Ollama or LM Studio
installed at all, by fetching weights from HuggingFace ourselves.

It is not a separate choice. LM Studio already downloads from HuggingFace, so
fetching them ourselves without an embedded engine only means handing the files
back to LM Studio to run. The two travel together, and the reasons are the ones
Part C already lists -- control and self-containment -- plus two this adds:

- **The repository carries the metadata Part B wants.** `config.json` gives the
  layers, KV heads and head dimension that Part D's bytes-per-token follows
  from, and the trained maximum window; the tokenizer and its chat template come
  with it. That is loading a model without a probe, from files, before anything
  is loaded into memory.
- **A revision is an unambiguous artifact.** MLX and GGUF are separate
  repositories with separate revisions, so the ambiguity PWR has to refuse
  today -- one LM Studio key serving two variants -- cannot arise, and the
  revision is a better digest than anything a campaign records now.

Its cost is a downloader that resumes and verifies twenty-gigabyte transfers,
disk management, licence-gated repositories needing an accepted agreement or a
token, and two catalogues of repositories because MLX and llama.cpp take
different formats. It is therefore part of open decision 4, not ahead of it.

### Typed local decisions instead of generated ones

Noted 2026-09-17 from an outside product, TypeSafe's Jev, which returns typed
probabilistic decisions -- choice, score -- in one pass instead of generating
text. **Jev itself is out of scope**: it is a hosted proprietary service behind
a waitlist, and `docs/vision-and-scope.md` puts remote providers out of scope;
it also generates nothing, so it could only ever be a component beside the
model, never instead of it.

The idea under it transfers, and is cheap. The loop makes many small decisions
by generating text from a large model -- is this file relevant, compact now or
not, is this check red, is the task finished. Those are choices over a fixed set
and can be taken as a constrained decoding over a couple of tokens on the model
already loaded, or by a small local classifier. It belongs in A4 and A5, where
the decision it replaces is already measured, and it is another instance of the
same argument as constrained tool calls: what may be produced is a property of
decoding, not of weights.

## Part D — the window follows the hardware, and models are catalogued by it

PWR will run on machines from 16 GB laptops to workstations, so neither the
A6 window nor the model can be fixed.

**Window.** Decided: A6 runs at the largest window the host sustains, and the
product does the same on any host. Without a probe that maximum is computed,
then confirmed by use:

- *Memory ceiling*: KV-cache bytes per token follow from the model's config
  (layers x KV heads x head dimension x 2 x bytes per element, less for
  sliding-window or hybrid-attention layers); the window is what fits in the
  memory left after the weights and a reserve for the OS and the app, capped at
  the model's trained maximum.
- *Latency ceiling*: prefill time grows with the window. Observed prefill speed
  from real turns caps the window at a first-token time the user accepts; until
  there is an observation, a conservative default per memory tier applies.
- The window in force, and which ceiling set it, are shown to the user and
  recorded with every campaign.

**Catalogue.** Models are classified by the hardware they need, not only by
size, and the classes are what the app recommends from:

| Host tier (unified or VRAM + RAM) | Model class, illustrative | Expected window |
|---|---|---|
| 16 GB | small, ~4-9B at 4-bit | modest |
| 32 GB | medium, ~14-30B at 4-bit, or MoE with few active parameters | medium |
| 64 GB (this Mac, M2 Max) | medium to large, ~30-35B at 4-8-bit, large MoE at 3-4-bit | large |
| 96 GB and above | large, 70B dense or large MoE at higher precision | large |

Each catalogue entry is a (model, quantisation, engine) with its weights'
memory, KV bytes per token, trained maximum window and measured speed on the
reference hosts, and the areas A1-A6 it has been evaluated in. The tiers and
examples above are placeholders to be replaced by measurements.

**Models.** Decided in outline, named later: evaluation starts with a medium
model (~30B) and also covers smaller and larger ones -- qwen3-coder-next among
the larger -- so a result can be read against the hardware it needs. A
harness decision that only holds for the model this Mac runs is not a product
decision. Testing a 16 GB class needs either a 16 GB machine or a memory cap
the engine enforces on this one; which is open.

## Part E — the largest single margin is the window, and it was set by a bad number

Established 2026-09-17, after the spike, and it reframes everything above.

`HISTORY_BUDGET_SHARE` in `pwr-orchestrator` is 0.5: the message history may
occupy half the context before the loop compacts. At the old regime's 16,384
tokens that is 8,192, less about 3,000 tokens of learned prompt overhead --
**roughly 5,000 tokens of memory for the agent**. The development run shows what
that does: 35 trials of 35 compacted at least twice, `bottle-json-config`
compacted 30 times in 40 actions, 15 trials died on the stall guard having
changed neither the workspace nor the checks in three windows of six actions,
and 2 of 35 resolved.

That window was not chosen; it was inherited from a calibration that recorded
7 tokens/s at 16k. The spike measured 44 (GGUF) and 65 (MLX) on the same
machine, and fitted the whole 262k window in 64 GB at a 49 GB peak. The harness
has been starved to avoid a slowness that was never there.

Three levers follow, in descending order of expected effect, and none of them
is a new subsystem:

1. **The working window**, once Part B stops gating on the probe.
2. **`HISTORY_BUDGET_SHARE`**, a one-line constant. Half the window for history
   is a reasonable reserve at 16k and waste at 128k.
3. **The action budget and the stall guard**, both sized under the old regime:
   40 actions is what a starved run needed, not what a task needs.

Until these are measured at a realistic window, no other intervention -- policy,
engine, adapter, retrieval -- can be credited or dismissed, because every
measurement of it would be taken under starvation.

## Impact on the roadmap

- **R2** stays COMPLETED as an old-regime measurement; its completion results
  are not carried forward as capability claims.
- **R3 is shelved** (decided 2026-09-17) for the duration of the refactor. When
  it resumes it splits: **R3-A5** tests H2's mechanism at a small window with
  mechanism metrics (`longhorizon-v2` and its injections serve it unchanged);
  **R3-A6** asks whether the retained policy helps completion at the host's
  window, after the A6 suite exists. The 60% share, three arms and one
  deployment are proposals to restate then.
- **S3** gains the engine: preparing a model in the app means choosing a window,
  not running a probe.
- **Windows** needs the embedded engine and execution isolation both.

## Proposed order of work

1. Label the old regime in the roadmap and experiment log (done).
2. Engine spike (Part C): MLX in-process against today's LM Studio path, on
   one medium model (done, 2026-09-17).
3. **The outside reference, first and cheapest** (revised 2026-09-17, moved up
   from step 6): five A6-shaped tasks run by hand in Bionic with the same model.
   Half a day, and it decides where every later hour goes. If Bionic finishes
   them, the gap is PWR's configuration and Part E says where to dig; if it
   does not, the tasks or the model are the limit and the harness is exonerated.
   No other step de-risks as many decisions for as little.
4. **Model loading without a probe** (Part B) with the computed window
   (Part D): the working window from metadata and a setting, speed observed
   from real turns, calibration demoted to a diagnostic.
5. **Re-measure at a realistic window** (Part E): the same tasks at the host's
   window, with `HISTORY_BUDGET_SHARE`, the action budget and the stall guard
   revisited. This is the measurement that says whether anything below matters.
6. A1-A4 suites, small and fast, re-auditing existing tasks into them.
7. Catalogue measurements: small, medium and large models on this Mac.
8. A6 suite at the host's window, against the step 3 reference.
9. The `Engine` boundary and the MLX engine, with weights from HuggingFace, if
   open decision 4 settles that way.
10. R3, when resumed.

Steps 3 to 5 are the whole of the near-term margin. The engine moved below them
because the spike removed its speed argument, and fine-tuning is not in this
list at all -- see open decision 5.

## Decisions, 2026-09-17

1. **The six areas A1-A6 are accepted.**
2. **A6 window: the largest the host sustains**, computed from hardware and
   model (Part D), not fixed at a number.
3. **Engine: MLX first** on macOS; llama.cpp behind the same boundary for
   Windows. The spike then showed LM Studio's MLX path as fast as in-process
   MLX, so building the embedded engine now is reopened (open decision 4).
4. **Models**: start from a medium ~30B model, and test smaller and larger ones
   (qwen3-coder-next among them); a hardware catalogue classifies them. Named
   models are chosen later.
5. **R3 is shelved** during the refactor.
6. **Embedded engines, then no HTTP backends** (2026-09-18, settling open
   decision 4's direction): MLX and GGUF run in PWR itself, and once they
   work well Ollama and LM Studio are removed rather than kept as options.
   **Its minimal form moved up on 2026-09-18** to right after the first
   diagnostic: A.17 showed that reasoning can be controlled only by whoever
   renders the chat template, that LM Studio's endpoint silently ignores the
   request to switch it off, and that runaway reasoning confounds every
   measurement of PWR through it. The speed argument is still absent; the
   reason now is control, then self-containment.

## Open decisions

1. Needle-recall check: keep it once per model, quantisation and window as a
   catalogue measurement, or drop it with the probe? Trade-offs are in Part B.
2. How the Rust core reaches MLX: Python sidecar or C API bindings (the spike).
3. How the 16 GB class is tested: a 16 GB machine, or an engine-enforced memory
   cap on this one.
4. *Direction decided 2026-09-18 (Decisions, 6); timing still follows the
   order of work.* Build the embedded MLX engine now -- together with fetching weights from
   HuggingFace, which is the same decision -- or keep LM Studio's MLX path as
   the default until constrained decoding or self-containment is needed.
5. **Fine-tuning or a LoRA adapter: when, and for what?** Raised 2026-09-17.
   *Not for tool calls*: the development run had one malformed call in 1,027
   turns, and a grammar makes an invalid call impossible where an adapter only
   makes it unlikely -- the control wanted is a property of decoding, not of
   weights. *Not from our own traces*: 2 of 35 trials resolved, so training on
   them would distil a weak policy; public agentic tool-use datasets avoid the
   licence question but teach a generic format, which is the part already
   working. *Possibly for behaviour a grammar cannot impose* -- reading a
   verifier before declaring completion, not re-reading a file already in
   context, keeping to PWR's protocol -- which is areas A2 and A4. It cannot
   be judged before those suites exist, and it would add a fourth dimension to a
   catalogue already keyed by model, quantisation and engine. Proposed: revisit
   after step 6, never before step 5.
6. **Quantisation strategy: a larger model at 4-bit, or a smaller one at 8-bit,
   at equal memory?** Raised 2026-09-17 from published work rather than from our
   own measurement. 8-bit is close to lossless; 4-bit loses most on exactly the
   long-context and agentic behaviour PWR depends on, and a ~14B at 8-bit
   costs about what a ~30B at 4-bit costs. Every plan here has silently assumed
   the larger model at 4-bit, and the engine spike measured that case. Proposed:
   carry a pair at equal memory in the catalogue and let A1 and A5 decide rather
   than assume it. Sources and the argument are in [`backlog.md`](backlog.md),
   Part 0b, which also gives a third ceiling for the working window -- published
   effective context, because a trained maximum is optimistic -- and bears on
   open decision 1.
